## Repair priorities

### 1. The central hypothesis does not yet make a clear directional prediction
You write that people who are “**poorly calibrated** give advice less weight.” Poor calibration can mean overconfidence, underconfidence, or both. Those states could plausibly predict *different* advice use. As written, the hypothesis does not explain why either kind of miscalibration should produce *less* advice uptake.

**Why reviewers will care:** The project’s main claim may be too vague to test, or may collapse into a simpler claim about confidence level, accuracy, or cognitive ability.

**Repair:** Define calibration and its direction precisely. State what will be measured (for example, the discrepancy between confidence and accuracy), how you will distinguish it from confidence level and metacognitive sensitivity, and why the predicted relationship with advice weighting should be negative. If you cannot justify one directional prediction, revise the question to test a more specific, defensible relationship.

### 2. You cannot yet establish that the project is original
The draft itself says, “**How far this goes beyond existing work is [?]**.” That is not a minor citation gap: novelty is one of the proposal’s central claims, and your interview summary says the supplied literature was simulated.

**Why reviewers will care:** Until you check the literature, they cannot tell whether this is a new question, a useful extension, or a replication of existing advice-driven recalibration work.

**Repair:** Replace the placeholder only after checking real, relevant literature. The state-of-research section should identify roughly five relevant publications and say specifically what they establish, what they leave unresolved, and what your project adds. Do not cite or rely on the simulated sources.

### 3. The proposed evidence may not support the causal language
The draft asks whether feedback “**improves**” calibration and whether improvement is “**followed by**” greater advice uptake. But a change over repeated sessions does not by itself show that feedback caused the change; nor does improvement preceding uptake establish that the former caused the latter. Repetition, task learning, changing advice quality, or other time-varying factors could explain the pattern.

**Why reviewers will care:** Your second study is meant to test the proposed relationship in both directions. Without a design that separates feedback effects from practice and time, it may show co-change without testing the mechanism.

**Repair:** In the methods, specify exactly what feedback participants receive, what outcomes and advice are held constant, and whether there is a suitable comparison condition or manipulation. Preregister how you will distinguish calibration change from general task learning and how you will test the temporal relationship. Until then, describe the result as an association, not a causal effect.

### 4. The key measures and the rival explanation are still too abstract
“**Advice weight**,” “**calibration**,” and “**accounting for accuracy**” are not operationalized here. Nor is “**General cognitive ability remains a rival explanation and will need to be addressed in the analysis**” enough to show how it will be addressed.

**Why reviewers will care:** A reviewer cannot judge whether the studies can answer the questions, or whether the main result would be distinguishable from differences in ability, confidence, or task performance.

**Repair:** In the methods, define the advice-weighting outcome and calibration measure, and explain how they are separated from confidence level and accuracy. Say how general cognitive ability will be measured or otherwise addressed; merely adding an unspecified statistical adjustment will not resolve the concern. The current “while accuracy remains similar” condition also needs a clear analytic meaning.

### 5. The significance is cautious but not yet specific to the field
“**It may clarify whether calibration helps explain advice use**” is accurate but generic. The draft explains why the topic follows your PhD; it does not yet show what important account of advice use could change if your hypothesis is supported.

**Why reviewers will care:** The committee must see the project’s significance beyond the fact that it extends your own research trajectory.

**Repair:** Once the literature check is complete, name the specific unresolved issue in advice-taking research and explain what each possible result would teach the field. Avoid inflating the claim; a narrow, well-supported contribution is stronger than a broad claim you cannot substantiate.
