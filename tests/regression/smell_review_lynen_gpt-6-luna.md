## Main problems, ranked

1. **The proposal reads as a continuation and geographic replication of your PhD, not yet as an independent research agenda.**  
   You write: “This project continues my doctoral thesis … and extends the same analysis to the US.” That raises the programme’s known concern about a direct thesis continuation. A US replication could be valuable, but the outline does not say what new explanatory question it answers or what existing account it could change. This affects **criterion 3** (originality and innovative potential) and **criterion 4** (future potential).  
   **Repair:** State the central research question and the specific theoretical or empirical gap the US comparison addresses. Explain why comparing these contexts changes what we can conclude—not simply whether the German findings recur in the US. Identify what is yours intellectually beyond the thesis.

2. **The plan is far too broad for 24 months, and the methods are not specified enough to judge feasibility.**  
   The aims include a survey, 60 interviews, a comparative database, a new theory, a monograph and a conference. “Survey and interviews, as in my thesis” does not tell reviewers how you will recruit participants in the US, ensure the samples are comparable, define platform work, or complete data collection and analysis in the fellowship period. This directly affects **criterion 3**, including focus, feasibility and viability at the host institute.  
   **Repair:** Reduce the project to a clear core study and a small number of deliverables. Specify the population, sampling and recruitment plan, comparison design, interview strategy, and what work will be completed during the fellowship. Present a monograph or conference as a possible later output, not as another central aim.

3. **The research outline does not yet make Stanford’s role credible.**  
   “Prof. Smith is world-leading and Stanford has excellent resources” is generic praise. Meeting the academic host once at a conference is not a problem in itself, but the draft gives no reason why this host or institute is necessary for the proposed research, or what expertise, access or intellectual exchange they will provide. This affects **criterion 3** (viability at the host institute) and **criterion 4** (career development).  
   **Repair:** Name the specific expertise or research environment you need, how it connects to the project’s methods or comparative design, and what you will gain that advances your independent profile. The host’s statement also needs to make clear how the proposal arose and whether you developed it independently.

4. **The state of research is both overlong and too broad for the instructions.**  
   Two pages and 23 references spanning inequality, platforms, labour markets, migration and education do not show a focused state of research. The programme guidance calls for approximately five relevant publications and a state-of-research section of no more than one page. This weakens **criterion 3** because the gap and contribution are hard to see.  
   **Repair:** Cut to the few studies directly needed to establish the specific gap. Organise the section around what is known, what remains unresolved, and how your question addresses that gap.

5. **The key-publication note does not show your individual contribution or establish the article’s significance convincingly.**  
   You say it was co-authored with your supervisor and two colleagues, but do not say what you personally designed, analysed, wrote or led. “Cited several times” is vague and does not, on its own, demonstrate impact. This affects **criterion 2** (quality of key publications, especially your contribution to multi-author work) and **criterion 1** (academic productivity). The explanation also looks likely to exceed the 1,000-character limit; check it with a character counter before submission.  
   **Repair:** Replace general claims with your specific contribution, the article’s precise original finding, and why that finding matters to your research profile. Avoid presenting the US project merely as the next step from this article.
