## Ranked issues

1. **The central claim’s novelty is still unknown.**  
   You write: “The closest published work and the precise novelty of this question remain to be verified [?].” This is the most serious problem. At Step 1, reviewers need to judge whether the project asks an important question and advances the frontier. As drafted, they cannot tell whether this is a genuinely new account of agreement attraction or a new combination of familiar elements. The open-point markers make the section visibly unfinished.  
   **Repair:** Verify and cite the closest work, then state the specific unresolved result or limitation it leaves behind. Make the distinction explicit: what do you explain that existing multilingual-model, agreement-attraction, or child-scale learning studies do not? Do not claim a gap broader than the literature supports.

2. **The hypothesis does not yet specify a clean test of case syncretism.**  
   The proposal says errors may occur “where ambiguous case cues favour a nearby noun,” and Objective 2 asks whether errors “vary with cue ambiguity across languages.” But cross-language differences could reflect word order, agreement morphology, training data, task design, or frequency—not syncretism. “The set will be fixed only after checking” corpus availability and contrasts also leaves it unclear whether the decisive comparison is already defined.  
   **Repair:** State what counts as an ambiguous cue, how it is measured, and what contrast would support or falsify the hypothesis. Explain how the design separates syncretism from language-level differences—ideally through controlled item-level contrasts, not just a comparison across six languages. Specify the language-selection criteria before inspecting results.

3. **“Human-like error patterns” is not yet a sufficiently precise outcome.**  
   The section wisely says that matching errors do not establish human-like processing. But the project still needs to define what match it will test: error rates, which attractor conditions, direction of the effect, or a pattern across conditions? The human comparison is central to the framing, yet you say the datasets’ suitability remains open.  
   **Repair:** Name the behavioural signature and the human evidence needed to test it. Clarify whether human data are a core test of the hypothesis or a contextual comparison. If comparable data do not exist for the selected languages or stimuli, the project’s claim about “human-like” errors needs to be narrowed.

4. **Objective 3 risks treating probing as evidence of mechanism.**  
   “Probing and activation patching” names tools, but not yet a convincing inference. A probe can decode information without showing that the model relies on it; patching can alter predictions for reasons other than the proposed representation. The stated result—an effect from patching nearby cues but little effect from patching subject–verb relations—would be informative only if the representations, locations and controls are defined.  
   **Repair:** State what is patched, where and against which counterfactual, and what controls would rule out generic disruption or position effects. Explain what result would count against the local-cue account, not only what would favour it.

5. **Your prior result is relevant but underused.**  
   “My earlier work found that agreement errors across 14 languages tracked word frequency rather than syntactic distance” gives the project a credible starting point and sets up a rival explanation. But the section does not say how the new project moves beyond that finding or how frequency will be controlled in the new tests.  
   **Repair:** Briefly identify the specific advance from that result to this proposal, and state how frequency enters the test design. The full PI case belongs elsewhere, but this section should make the intellectual progression legible.

## How a Step 1 reviewer is likely to read this

A sympathetic reviewer will see a promising question and appreciate the caution about equating similar errors with similar cognition. A sceptical reviewer will see a **promising idea whose novelty, decisive contrast and observable predictions are still being worked out**. The section currently asks the panel to accept the project’s importance before it has established what the nearest research already shows.

Do not spend Part I space on unresolved corpus logistics. Corpus availability and implementation belong mainly in Part II. But the choice of languages and contrasts is part of the scientific argument: Part I must show why those cases can answer the question.

- The hypothesis is falsifiable in principle: similar patterns without the proposed ambiguity would challenge it.
