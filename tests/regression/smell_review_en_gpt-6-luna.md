## Main weaknesses, ranked

1. **The independence case is currently the central problem.**  
   The project “builds directly on the computational framework developed in Prof. Müller’s group during my PhD and continues its line of research”; you have remained in the same group as a postdoc, and Müller would mentor you and provide the cluster. A reviewer could read this as a continuation of the supervisor’s programme, with the applicant still dependent on the former supervisor for intellectual direction and essential resources. That is a major concern for an Emmy Noether Group, whose purpose is to establish an independent research line.  
   **Repair:** Specify what intellectual and technical elements are yours, what will be new beyond Müller’s programme, and what decisions you will control independently. Show evidence of that independence in your publication contributions, project leadership, collaborations, funding, and access to resources. Explain how the group can operate without relying on Müller’s ongoing involvement.

2. **The host choice is not justified, and it compounds the independence concern.**  
   Heidelberg is both your PhD institution and postdoc workplace. “Excellent infrastructure” does not explain why this is the right host for an independent group, or how the arrangement avoids continued dependence on the former supervisor.  
   **Repair:** Give specific reasons for Heidelberg and for this department, explain what the host provides beyond Müller’s lab, and set out practical safeguards for scientific and managerial independence. Address why a different host is not preferable. The employer’s statement and the proposed working conditions will matter too.

3. **The work programme is broad, but its central scientific question is not visible.**  
   The six goals range from adapting and validating a framework to new applications, a web tool, industry collaborations, and machine-learning extensions. As presented, this is a list of activities rather than a hypothesis-led programme. The riskiest step—whether the existing framework can be adapted successfully to membrane proteins—has no stated preliminary evidence here.  
   **Repair:** Identify the main hypothesis, the decisive tests, and the limits of the project. Make clear what result would count as success or failure. Separate core aims from optional extensions; the web tool, industry work, and machine learning need a clear scientific rationale and a justified place in the plan.

4. **The duration and staffing do not yet add up to a credible group plan.**  
   A five-year plan is not aligned with the usual six-year Emmy Noether structure of **3 + 3 years**, with an interim evaluation at the end of year 3. You list three PhD students, two postdocs, and a technician, but provide no task allocation, supervision plan, or justification for this team size.  
   **Repair:** Structure the work and milestones across the two funding phases. Link every requested position to defined work, dependencies, and outputs. Explain how you will supervise doctoral researchers, build and lead the group, and contribute to teaching. Without this, reviewers cannot judge whether the group is feasible or whether you are ready to lead it.

5. **The track record and international experience are presented in ways that weaken the case.**  
   All nine papers have Prof. Müller as last author. That does not establish your role, and impact factors do not demonstrate publication quality to DFG reviewers. A two-month ETH visit may also look limited against the expectation of substantial international research experience; the separate appendix matters.  
   **Repair:** For relevant papers, state your specific contribution and identify your own work within the publication list. Replace impact-factor arguments with evidence from the work itself. In the international-experience appendix, explain what you did, what scientific independence or network resulted, and why the experience is substantial. Two months is a concern, not by itself proof of ineligibility.

## Likely reviewer reading

A review-board member from outside the subfield may see a group that remains closely attached to the former supervisor’s programme, at the same institution, with substantial staffing but no visible leadership plan. That combination would make the applicant’s readiness to lead an independent Emmy Noether Group difficult to endorse.
