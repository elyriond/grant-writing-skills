## Scope and assumptions

This is **partial material**: a short draft of sections 1–2 and a headline budget, not a reviewable project description. I assume this is a **first submission**; you have not said whether it is a resubmission. My knowledge refers to DFG forms version **09/26** and may be outdated.

## Ranked issues

1. **There is no research question or testable hypothesis.** “Explore the role of urban green spaces” does not specify what role, for which pollinators, through which mechanisms, or compared with what. “Generate new insights” is an aspiration, not an objective. A reviewer cannot judge originality or expected knowledge gain (review question 1), or whether the work programme can deliver it (question 2).  
   **Repair:** State a bounded question, explicit hypotheses and predictions, and the specific gap in current knowledge. Explain briefly why the answer is not already available in the literature. Define the study system, outcomes and comparison.

2. **The work programme is not a research design.** “We will collect data on pollinators,” “standard methods,” and analyses “as appropriate” leave sampling, replication, variables, analysis and decision points unspecified. The schedule also does not show how fieldwork spans relevant seasons or what happens if access, weather or sampling fails. This prevents assessment of method suitability, feasibility and schedule (question 2).  
   **Repair:** Specify sites and selection, taxa, sampling units and frequency, field methods, measured predictors and outcomes, sample-size rationale or power analysis where appropriate, planned statistical models, permits/access status, milestones, fallback plans and outputs. Allocate work by season, not just by broad month ranges.

3. **The preliminary-work and independence case is absent.** “Our group has published extensively” does not establish your contribution, your preparation to lead this project, or independence from the group. The journal impact factors are not evidence DFG reviewers should use. This is particularly important for an Eigene Stelle applicant (question 3).  
   **Repair:** Identify relevant preliminary results and your specific role in them; show how they support the proposed design. In the CV and proposal, make your contributions and the project’s independence from the host group explicit. The quality and relevance of the actual publications matter, not their impact factors.

4. **Several required or material elements are missing.** The summary is English-only; the research-data section is blank; and the bibliography highlights 12 own works, above the limit of 10 highlighted own works per proposal. The draft also does not establish the work environment, employer support or access to the field sites.  
   **Repair:** Provide both German and English summaries; complete the research-data section with data types, formats, storage, access, archiving and any restrictions; reduce highlighted own works to at most 10. Describe relevant facilities and permissions. For the Eigene Stelle, include the required employer statement (41.027). Given the Leibniz host and your three years since the doctorate, verify whether the applicable institutional contribution requirement applies; the non-university-institute rule may be relevant. The stated fixed-term employment does not appear to trigger the separate rule for permanent staff, but confirm your status and the current requirements with the institute/DFG.

5. **The case for the requested resources is not linked to tasks.** The budget gives no hours or duties for the student assistant, no breakdown of consumables, and no justification for six conferences or the equipment bundle. This hits the assessment of whether funds are justified and reasonable (question 5), as well as the requirement that work correspond to funds requested.  
   **Repair:** Tie every item to a defined task, quantity and calculation. Explain why the work cannot be done with existing facilities or basic equipment. See item-by-item comments below.

### Prose check

These sentences read as generic or promotional, regardless of how they were drafted:

- “In today's rapidly evolving landscape of urban ecology, it is no secret that pollinators are crucial.”
- “Urban green spaces are truly pivotal for biodiversity.”
- “The project will explore the role of urban green spaces for pollinators and generate new insights into urban biodiversity.”

Replace them with specific claims about the state of knowledge, the gap, and the project’s testable contribution. Also remove the impact-factor claim: it does not establish the quality of the preliminary work and is likely to distract from the evidence reviewers need.
