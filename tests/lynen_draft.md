Research outline
1. State of research (2 pages, 23 references): A broad overview of the literature on social inequality, digital platforms, labour markets, migration and education.
2. Research questions: This project continues my doctoral thesis on platform work in Germany and extends the same analysis to the US. Aims: (1) replicate my thesis survey in the US; (2) conduct 60 interviews; (3) build a comparative database; (4) develop a new theory of platform labour; (5) write a monograph; (6) organise an international conference.
3. Methods: Survey and interviews, as in my thesis.
Why Stanford: Prof. Smith is world-leading and Stanford has excellent resources.

Key publication 1 – explanation (to be pasted into the 1,000-character field):
This article, published in a leading journal, is the central publication of my doctoral thesis and presents the main survey results on platform workers in Germany, showing that platform work is associated with lower income stability, fewer social protections and higher stress compared with standard employment, which has important implications for policy and for the theory of labour market segmentation, a field that has been widely discussed in the literature for decades and remains highly relevant today given the rapid growth of platform work in Europe and beyond. The article was co-authored with my supervisor and two colleagues and has already been cited several times, which demonstrates its impact on the field. It shows my ability to conduct large-scale survey research, manage complex datasets, and publish in highly competitive outlets, and it forms the basis of the proposed project at Stanford, which will extend these findings to the US context.
