## Fatal weaknesses in the material shown

1. **The novelty claim is currently unsupported.** Your own text says, “How far this goes beyond existing work is **[?]**,” and the five supplied sources were simulated. That is the central weakness of a *Questions and significance* section: a field reviewer cannot tell whether the question advances the field or repeats an established result. **Repair:** verify the literature before settling the claim. Identify the nearest studies on calibration, advice weighting and feedback-driven recalibration; then say precisely what your studies test that they do not.

2. **The hypothesis and the key publication do not yet connect as cleanly as the prose implies.** The proposed predictor is *calibration*—the fit between confidence and accuracy across decisions. The paper, as described, concerns **lower confidence after errors** and information-seeking. Those are related, but not interchangeable. “The paper grounds my proposed shift” skips the inferential step. **Repair:** explain what the paper establishes about your ability to study confidence beyond accuracy, and what remains untested about calibration and advice. Do not present the publication as prior evidence for the proposed hypothesis.

3. **The second question promises a stronger inference than its description supports.** “This tests the proposed link in both directions” is too strong. If feedback, calibration and advice uptake all change with practice, their order alone will not establish that improved calibration caused greater uptake. Nor does “accuracy remains similar” rule out other learning effects. **Repair:** in the methods section, specify how calibration and advice weighting will be measured over time, what comparison isolates the effect of feedback, and what result would count *against* the proposed account. Resolve the **[?]** fallback: if feedback does not improve calibration, the study should still have a clearly stated, interpretable result.
