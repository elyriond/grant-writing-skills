# Humboldt Foundation: Feodor Lynen Research Fellowship for postdocs
Source: programme information (01/2026), information for hosts, AI statement (12/2025). Check humboldt-foundation.de for newer versions.

## Fit check
- "People, not projects": the applicant's academic qualification decides, relative to career stage.
- At most 4 years between the final doctoral step (defence/viva) and receipt of the application; recognised career breaks extend this to max. 6. State the result explicitly and ask about breaks.
- No publication at all: formal rejection.
- German citizens based in Germany, or non-Germans integrated in the German science system.
- At most 6 months in the host country within the last 18 months. No earlier Humboldt sponsorship.
- Host: a Humboldt Network member established abroad (former sponsoree, host of one, selection-committee member, Frontiers of Research Symposium participant) or a holder of a listed international award. Not only alumni. If the host's status is unclear, ask the applicant to confirm it with the host or the Foundation.
- 6–24 months; up to three stays of at least 3 months, the first at least 6. No deadlines; the committee meets in February, June and October; submit 4–7 months ahead. About 40% succeed.
- Better fit: project in Germany: DFG Sachbeihilfe with Eigene Stelle. Own group: Emmy Noether or ERC Starting Grant.
- Ask: doctorate date and breaks; citizenship and residence; host and Network status; time spent in the host country; whether thesis results are published.

## Documents and budget
- Research outline, max. 5 pages incl. bibliography. It must answer:
  - "What is the current state of research (evidenced by approx. five relevant publications from the research area; one page max)?"
  - "What questions are to be examined? What is the significance of these questions for the advancement of the research area?"
  - "What scientific methods are to be used to achieve the objectives of the proposal…?"
  Suggested split (not a Humboldt rule): state of research 1; questions and significance 1.5; methods and feasibility at the host institute 2; bibliography 0.5. Headings are unconfirmed; check the online form.
- CV, max. 2 pages; publication list.
- 1–3 key publications, each explained in max. 1,000 characters: relevance, significance for the profile, own contribution.
- Uploaded by others: host's statement; two expert reviews (doctoral supervisor plus one other researcher).
- No project budget, no work packages.

## Selection criteria (official wording)
1. "academic career and academic record to date (mobility, determination, thematic scope, academic productivity)"
2. "quality of the key publications named in the application (originality, innovativeness, evaluation of your own contribution to publications involving several authors)"
3. "originality and innovative potential of your proposed research (significance for the further development of the subject area, convincing selection of scientific methods, potential for scientific development, clear focus and feasibility within the requested sponsorship period, viability at the host institute)"
4. "your future potential (academic potential, personal development, career prospects)"
An interdisciplinary selection committee decides: non-specialists must be able to follow.

## Extra interview topics
- How the contact with the host came about and how the proposal arose. The host is asked: "did the applicant prepare the submitted research project independently?"
- Why this host; what is possible there and not at home.
- How the project differs from the doctoral thesis.
- What the stay adds to the career.

## Pitfalls (derived from criteria and instructions)
- Outline reads as the host's project.
- Direct continuation of the doctoral thesis; thesis results unpublished.
- Generic host fit; scope too large for the months requested.
- Innovative idea not stated explicitly.
- More than 3 key publications; own contribution not explained.

## AI disclosure: not required
"For the time being, we do not require applicants to identify use" (statement 12/2025). The idea must still be visibly the applicant's own: in the close, remind the applicant that the host's statement must say whether they prepared the project independently.
